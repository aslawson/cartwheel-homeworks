Watching the failure rate after shipping

Part three

What I learned

The honest answer

The estimate moved, but nothing changed

The ranges overlap almost entirely: 0–58% before, 6–93% after. With ten conversations per period, one extra flag moves the corrected rate about 13 points. The right reading is “no detectable change”. It still crosses my threshold, and by my own rule that means: read the flagged conversations, confirm them by hand, and turn real failures — like support-0085 — into Homework 6 test cases. One crossing is weak evidence; act on a repeated one.

Why grader quality matters

A weak grader can't tell you anything — and hides real changes

Both graders flagged 2 of 10 before. The correction turned that into 19% for the good grader and 8% for mine, because mine false-alarms more, so its flags are discounted. Its range was 0–100% in both periods.

A raw before/after difference is the true difference × (catch rate − false-alarm rate).

For the reference grader that factor is 0.78; for mine, 0.27. A real 10-point drop would show up as about 3 points on mine — easy to miss.

A surprise in the risk group

Looking up a policy made failures rarer, not commoner

Conversations that looked up a policy were flagged 1 of 16 and 1 of 17 — less than the random sample. Partly the agent is better grounded with the policy in hand. Partly it's the grader: it counts only retrieved documents as support, so any policy claim made without a lookup is unsupported by design. For this failure, the risky conversations are the ones that state policy without checking. Pick risk groups by what causes the failure, not by topic.

Automating it

The daily monitor reads traffic; it never runs the agent

.github/workflows/monitor.yml · the daily monitor
on: schedule: [{ cron: "0 6 * * *" }] # every day, 06:00 UTC workflow_dispatch: # plus a manual button steps: - uv sync - uv run python -m monitoring.run --last-hours 24 - upload monitoring/output # kept even if the job fails

Each day it reads the last 24 hours of traces from Langfuse, groups them by session into conversations, and runs the same sampling, grading, correction and score writing. It needs only the Langfuse keys and the grader's key, stored as GitHub secrets. A quiet day records zero and calls no grader. My Langfuse runs on my laptop, so the job is configured for a self-hosted runner on that machine — a GitHub-hosted machine couldn't reach localhost.

Plumbing, briefly

Small fixes, written down

Langfuse rejects a score attached to nothing, so each period's rate is pinned to the period's last trace. Scores were first dated when written, which put both periods on one day; they're now dated to their conversations — and since Langfuse keeps a score's original date on update, the old ones had to be deleted and recreated. Its local worker deletes one score every two minutes by default, so I raised that limit. Re-sending a period's scores updates them in place rather than duplicating, as the assignment requires.

Where it ended up. The same 50 conversations graded in two periods by two frozen graders: reference 19% → 44%, mine 8% → 44%, with overlapping ranges. Scores and a dashboard in Langfuse, a daily monitor workflow, and a short write-up in monitoring/README.md.
